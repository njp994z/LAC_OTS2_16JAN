Nope, there isn’t some “output variable” you forgot to set. If Pass 1 still tops out around **486°C** while conversion looks about right, that almost always means the **energy balance is still not actually applying the right heat release per degree of temperature rise** (unit basis mismatch), or you’re still **plotting/printing a different temperature than the one being integrated**.

Here are the two big things to check next, in order of likelihood.

---

## 1) Your energy balance is still mixing unit bases (this will keep ΔT too small)

In your snippet earlier you had:

* `dHrxn_J_per_molSO2` in **J/mol**
* `FT_mol_s` in **mol/s**
* `r` and `dX_dW = r / F_SO2_0` strongly suggests `r` is on a **lbmol/hr** basis (since `F_SO2_0` is lbmol/hr)

If `r` is lbmol/hr and you divide by `FT_mol_s` (mol/s), your `dT_dW` will be wrong by a big factor and your outlet temperature will stay too low even if Cp is perfect.

### The clean, low-drama fix

Do the whole energy balance in **BTU/lbmol·R** and **lbmol/hr** (so you don’t need mol/s anywhere):

```ts
// dH: J/mol -> BTU/lbmol
const dHrxn_BTU_per_lbmolSO2 = dHrxn_J_per_molSO2 * 0.195305; // (453.59237 mol/lbmol) * (1 BTU/1055.056 J)

// Cp_mix: BTU/lbmol-R from your polynomial database
const Cp_mix_BTU_per_lbmolR = cp_mix_BTU_lbmolR(TC, y_local);

// r should be lbmol/hr per catalyst weight (same basis you use in dX_dW)
const FT_lbmol_hr = F_T0;

// gives dT/dW in °R per (catalyst weight unit)
const dT_dW_R = (-(dHrxn_BTU_per_lbmolSO2) * r) / (FT_lbmol_hr * Cp_mix_BTU_per_lbmolR);

// integrate temperature in °C
const dT_dW_C = dT_dW_R / 1.8;
```

If you instead keep everything in SI, that’s fine too, but then **you must convert `r` to mol/s** to match `FT_mol_s`. Mixing is what causes your exact symptom.

Also double-check you didn’t accidentally use **4186.8** instead of **4.1868** when converting BTU/lbmol·R → J/mol·K. A 1000× Cp inflation will absolutely pin your outlet temperature down near where you are.

---

## 2) You might be displaying the wrong temperature field (UI wiring issue)

Even if the integrator is producing the right outlet T, the table can still show an older or different variable if it’s bound to, say:

* `seg.T_out_C` that never got updated
* the last “profile point” from a previous run
* a per-pass “inlet temp” variable reused in rendering

Quick sanity check:

* Wherever you compute the final RK4 arrays (`T[i]`, `X[i]`, etc.), log the last entry:

  * `console.log("Pass1 Tout", T[T.length-1])`
* Then compare to what the table prints.

If console says ~618°C but table says 486°C, it’s purely a display mapping bug.
